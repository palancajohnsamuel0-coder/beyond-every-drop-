01 / INTRODUCTION

Water is more than a resource.

Water is essential to life, ecosystems, agriculture, industry, and human development. Every community depends on reliable access to clean and safe water.

But water does not simply appear when we turn on a faucet. It moves through natural cycles and engineered systems that collect, treat, distribute, use, and manage it.

71%

of Earth's surface

is covered by water. However, most of it is salt water, and only a small portion is readily accessible freshwater.

02 / WHERE WATER BEGINS

Understanding Water Sources

Water comes from different natural sources. Understanding them helps us protect and manage our freshwater resources.

Surface Water

Water found on Earth's surface, including rivers, lakes, reservoirs, and streams. It is commonly used for public water supply after treatment.

Groundwater

Water stored beneath the ground in soil pores and rock formations called aquifers. Wells can provide access to this freshwater source.

Springs

Natural places where groundwater flows out onto Earth's surface. Spring water still requires quality assessment.

Rainwater

Water collected from rainfall, often from rooftops. Proper collection, storage, and treatment are important before use.

03 / NATURE'S SYSTEM

The Water Cycle

Water continuously moves between the atmosphere, land, and bodies of water through natural processes.

01. Evaporation

Heat from the sun changes liquid water into water vapor.

02. Condensation

Water vapor cools and forms tiny droplets in clouds.

03. Precipitation

Water falls back to Earth as rain or other forms.

04. Collection

Water gathers in rivers, lakes, oceans, and underground.

04 / PROTECTING OUR RESOURCES

Water Quality and Contamination

Water pollution can make water unsafe for people and ecosystems.

Industrial Waste

Improper industrial discharges can introduce harmful substances into waterways.

Improper Waste Disposal

Garbage and plastics can pollute rivers, drainage systems, and coastal environments.

Agricultural Runoff

Fertilizers, pesticides, and animal waste can enter water bodies through runoff.

Untreated Wastewater

Pathogens and pollutants in untreated wastewater can threaten public health.

Clear water is not always safe water.

Some contaminants cannot be seen, smelled, or tasted. Water safety requires appropriate testing and treatment.

05 / ENGINEERING FOR LIFE

How Water Is Treated

Drinking water treatment uses processes to remove contaminants and make water suitable for its intended use.

01

Intake and Screening

Water is collected and large debris is removed using screens.

02

Coagulation and Flocculation

Small suspended particles gather into larger clumps called flocs.

03

Sedimentation

Heavier flocs settle at the bottom of a treatment basin.

04

Filtration

Water passes through filter materials to remove remaining particles.

05

Disinfection

Disinfection reduces disease-causing microorganisms.

06

Storage and Distribution

Treated water is stored and delivered through distribution systems.

06 / HEALTH AND THE ENVIRONMENT

Sanitation Protects Water

Proper sanitation prevents human waste and other pollutants from contaminating water sources.

Reduce Disease

Proper sanitation helps prevent the spread of waterborne diseases.

Protect Groundwater

Safe sanitation systems help reduce contamination of groundwater.

Protect Ecosystems

Wastewater management helps protect rivers and aquatic life.

Manage Wastewater

Wastewater treatment removes or reduces pollutants before discharge or reuse.

07 / OUR SHARED RESPONSIBILITY

Every Drop Has a Future

Sustainable water management means using water responsibly while protecting resources for future generations.

Conserve Water

Fix leaks, turn off taps when not needed, and use water efficiently.

Protect Watersheds

Support forests and natural areas that help protect water sources.

Prevent Pollution

Dispose of chemicals and household waste properly.

Take Part

Support community cleanups and water education.
